Model · 1

Why a model, and what it predicts Planned

The map today shows history: in which months people have logged chanterelles around each city over all years. It cannot say whether this autumn will be good. Chanterelles fruit after the soil has been warm and wet for a few weeks, so a wet August usually means a good September and a dry summer means a poor one.

The model will answer one question for a chosen city and month: how likely is it that this month will be a better-than-usual chanterelle month there, given the weather of the past weeks?

Status: not trained yet. Every result number on this page that would come from the model is n/s (not shown) until it exists.

Model · 2

Which model, and its inputs and outputs Planned

Inputs (per city, per month)

  • Rain in the previous 1, 2 and 3 months, and how far each is above or below normal
  • Mean temperature in the previous 1 and 2 months
  • Soil moisture and days since the last heavy rain (ERA5-Land reanalysis)
  • Month of the year and latitude
  • Forest share around the city
  • Search effort: all fungi records logged in the same area and month (so a busy observer year is not mistaken for a good mushroom year)

Output

A probability from 0 to 1 that the month is above that city's usual level of chanterelle sightings, after adjusting for search effort.

On the map this would drive the fox's bag: a full bag would mean "very likely a good month".

Models

  • Baseline: climatology, the city's historical share of sightings in that month
  • Model 1: logistic regression (easy to read: each input gets one weight)
  • Model 2: gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM), kept only if it clearly beats model 1

How it will be trained, tested and evaluated Planned

1 · DataGBIF sightings per city, per year and month, 2000–2025, plus monthly weather for the same months
2 · TrainYears 2000–2019
3 · ValidateYears 2020–2022, used to tune settings
4 · TestYears 2023–2025, touched once at the end
  • Split by year, not at random. A random split would let the model see a neighbouring city in the same wet September and look better than it really is.
  • Spatial check: leave one country out at a time, to see if a model trained on Sweden and Germany still works for, say, France or Oregon.
  • Same rule for everyone: a model only counts as useful if it beats the climatology baseline on the test years.
  • Needed first: the current grid file pools all years together. Training needs a new fetch with a year breakdown (GBIF facet=year per cell and month) and a daily weather series per city.

Data · 1

Chanterelle sightings (GBIF) Collected

What GBIF is. The Global Biodiversity Information Facility is an international, government-funded open data network. It does not collect records itself. It gathers "a species was here on this date" records from thousands of sources into one standard format: museum and herbarium collections, national species atlases (for example Artportalen in Sweden, the Danish fungal atlas, the British Mycological Society), and citizen-science apps such as iNaturalist and Mushroom Observer. Each record has a species, coordinates and usually a date.

How the data was fetched. Through the official GBIF occurrence API (api.gbif.org/v1/occurrence/search), with a script (scripts/fetch_grid_fast.py) run in November 2026. The same query was used for all three regions; only the area filter changes.

  • Genus Cantharellus (taxonKey=9623860), so golden chanterelle and its close relatives
  • Only records with coordinates (hasCoordinate=true) and no GBIF-flagged location problem (hasGeospatialIssue=false)
  • Europe by continent=EUROPE, the USA by country=US, Canada by country=CA
  • Counted, not downloaded: the area is cut into 1° × 1.5° cells (about 110 km), and GBIF returns the count per cell with a month breakdown (facet=month)

Cleaning done.

  • Records without coordinates or with flagged coordinates are excluded by the query
  • Records without a month count toward "All year" but not toward any month, so month totals can be smaller
  • In the earlier country and state counts, misspelled region names ("Illinios", "Nc", "Quebec", "Newfoundland") were merged into the right region

Not cleaned yet. No duplicate removal (the same find can be logged in two databases), no filter on coordinate precision, no year filter (all years are pooled), and no species split inside the genus. All record types are kept: human observations, museum specimens and a few literature records.

Data · 2

From grid cells to cities Estimated

People look for "near Stockholm", not "cell 59°N 18°E", so each cell's records are credited to one big city:

  • Big city = 150,000+ people (60,000+ in Canada, where cities are fewer)
  • Each cell goes to the most populous big city within about 110 km of the cell centre; if there is none, to the nearest big city within 160 km; otherwise to the largest town of 40,000+ people within 110 km; otherwise to the nearest town
  • Result: 260 cities in Europe, 153 in the USA, 42 in Canada, and every record is assigned exactly once
  • City list: GeoNames, through the open all-the-cities package (cities of 1,000+ people with population figures)

The 25 km ring on the map shows where to start looking around the city. The count itself covers the whole cell, so it reaches further than 25 km.

Data · 3

Rain and temperature Estimated

Source. Long-term monthly normals (mostly 1991–2020) from 86 weather stations: 39 in Europe, 34 in the USA, 13 in Canada. The numbers come from national weather services (for example SMHI, the Met Office, Deutscher Wetterdienst, NOAA, Environment Canada) as published in the climate tables of the Wikipedia article for each city.

How it was turned into data. The tables are text on web pages. An AI agent read each table and typed the 12 monthly values into the data file. Values for Copenhagen and several UK stations came from a search summary rather than the table itself, and the Inverness temperatures are still to be checked. These are marked as needing a check against the original service.

Rain contours. For each map point, the station values are blended with weights that fall off with distance (Gaussian, about 180 km), on a 0.4° grid, and drawn as contour bands. Points more than 450 km from any station are left blank.

Temperature. Shown only for 9 featured places that have a full climate table. For other cities the chart shows rain from the nearest station and no temperature.

These are averages over 30 years, not this year's weather. The model will need actual monthly weather per year (planned: ERA5-Land from the Copernicus Climate Data Store).

Limits

  • More records can mean more searchers. Europe has many more records partly because national atlases there are large and well used. A city with few records is not proof that chanterelles are rare there. Adjusting for search effort is the next step.
  • Cells are about 110 km wide. A city's count covers a large area, not one forest.
  • Climate is a 30-year average. It shows the usual pattern, not this year's.
  • Never eat a mushroom you have not identified with certainty. False chanterelles and jack-o'-lantern mushrooms look similar. Check with a local expert.
